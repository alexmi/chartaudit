# ChartAudit

Hindsight-free scoring for the lines traders draw on charts. ChartAudit reads a marked-up chart screenshot with Meta's Muse Spark, turns each drawn position box and level into exact prices and dates, and scores it only on what price did after the chart was made, compared with algorithmic and random lines.

Built during the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon, Oct 27 to Nov 4, 2026. Everything in this repository was written during the hackathon.

## Try it

The demo page (`app.py`, a Gradio app that runs as a Hugging Face Space) has two ways in:

* **Built-in examples, no upload needed.** Three synthetic charts with readings saved from Muse Spark: levels that held, levels that broke, and a long position box that hit its target. They use no model call and no price lookup, so they always work. The prices are a random walk, so a level that "held" here held by chance; that is the point of comparing with random levels.
* **Upload your own chart.** Muse Spark reads the price levels, zones and position boxes, ChartAudit checks the read price axis, scores each drawing on the daily prices after the chart's date, and draws what was read over those prices. Uploads are not stored. Reading is limited per visitor and per day.

One honest note from building the examples: Muse Spark read the first winning box chart upside down (stop and target swapped, so a long box became a short one) and read the next three correctly to within 0.4. The example uses one of the correct reads; the miss is recorded in `scripts/make_examples.py`. It is why every reading goes through the axis check before anything is scored.

Rebuild the examples with `python scripts/make_examples.py` (three model calls), or `--truth` to save the answer key instead.

## Results (Nov 4, 2026)

**Real charts:** 51 marked-up charts from one trader's published analysis on Sep 8, 2026 (images kept private). Muse Spark read all 51; 122 of the 186 drawn prices had no printed label and were estimated from the price axis. Daily prices are in hand for all 51. Of those, 43 have drawings to score, and all 43 passed the axis check.

Levels, scored only on the 20 trading days after each chart:

| | levels | reached | held | broke | hold rate | 95% interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drawn by the trader | 91 | 21 | 5 | 16 | 24% | 11% to 45% |
| Swing pivots (same charts) | 83 | 34 | 13 | 21 | 38% | 24% to 55% |
| Random, matched side and distance | 455 | 113 | 32 | 81 | 28% | 21% to 37% |
| Random, anywhere on the chart | 455 | 182 | 48 | 134 | 26% | 21% to 33% |

* **No edge.** Drawn levels held 24% of the time when price reached them, against 26% to 38% for pivot and random levels. The intervals overlap, so on this sample the drawn levels are neither better nor measurably worse than random ones.
* **Touches helped little.** Levels touched three or more times before the chart held 4 of 13 times (31%).
* **Zones:** 9 of 18 drawn zones that price reached held (50%), against 59% for random zones of the same width and 68% for zones at swing pivots.
* **Position boxes:** one was reached, and it lost.

This is one chart set and small samples, so it settles nothing about this trader or about chart lines in general. What it shows is that the question can now be asked of any set of marked-up charts, cheaply and without hindsight. Next: four more chart sets that are already read.

## Run the page

Locally: `.venv/bin/pip install -e ".[demo]"`, then `.venv/bin/python app.py`.

As a Hugging Face Space: create a Gradio Space, push this repository to it, and add Space secrets:

* `MODEL_API_KEY` (required for uploads; the built-in examples work without it)
* `ALPHAVANTAGE_API_KEY` (optional; daily prices for uploaded charts)

Optional settings: `LIVE_READS=off` turns model reading off without a redeploy; `READS_PER_DAY` (default 100), `READS_PER_VISITOR_HOUR` (default 5) and `PRICE_LOOKUPS_PER_DAY` (default 20) set the limits.

## Audit: scoring drawn lines on what happened next

`scripts/run_audit.py` takes chart readings plus a `charts.csv` (image id, ticker, chart date) and scores each drawing only on bars after the chart date:

* **Levels:** a touch is a bar within 0.25 ATR of the line; touches under 5 bars apart are one visit. A level *breaks* when a bar closes through it by more than that tolerance, and *holds* when price touches it without breaking, within 20 bars. Touches before the chart date are counted too, to test whether more touches mean a stronger level.
* **Position boxes:** fill on the first bar that trades through entry; win if the target comes before the stop within 20 bars; stop and target in the same bar count as a loss.
* **Zones (supply and demand):** a touch is a bar entering the band; a zone breaks on a close beyond its far edge, and a close inside the zone is not a break. A zone with price inside it on the chart date is not scored. Baselines are zones of the same width, centred on a swing pivot or placed at random.
* **Baselines:** on the same visible bars, swing pivot levels (one per drawn level) and random levels (five per drawn level). Hold rates carry 95% Wilson intervals.
* **Axis check:** before scoring, the read price axis must be self-consistent and the real closing price on the chart date must sit on it, and every drawn price must be on the axis. A chart that fails is set aside with the reason instead of being scored, because a misread axis would misplace every line.

Daily prices come from CSV files in `data/prices/` (any Date, Open, High, Low, Close export works) or, to fill gaps, the Alpha Vantage API with `ALPHAVANTAGE_API_KEY` in `.env`. Fetched prices are not committed.

## Trader calls from transcripts

`chartaudit.calls` sends a trading transcript to Muse Spark and gets back each specific call (ticker, buy, sell or watch, level or zone, timeframe, the exact quote) and each general rule the speaker states, such as how a level behaves on its first retest or after it breaks. On two transcripts it found 17 rules the audit can test. Most spoken calls point at a chart rather than naming a price, so they are joined to the chart readings. Transcripts are private and never committed.

## Model comparison (Oct 30, 2026)

The same 20 synthetic charts (known answers; 15 with a position box, 5 on a log scale), the same prompt and the same scorer:

| | Muse Spark 1.3 (Meta API) | Muse Spark Open (open weights, Baseten) | Claude Opus 5.5 | Claude Fable 5.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Every axis label right | 7 of 20 | 7 of 20 | 11 of 20 | 6 of 20 |
| Labeled lines read exactly | 45 of 45 | 45 of 45 | 45 of 45 | 45 of 45 |
| Log scale recognized | 0 of 5 | 0 of 5 | 5 of 5 | 0 of 5 |
| Unlabeled box prices given | 3, all wrong | 3, all wrong | none (null) | none (null) |
| Worst price error | 73 ticks | 58 ticks | 0 ticks | 0 ticks |

Notes:
* That prompt said "do not guess", so no model could score the box edges, which carry no printed price. The prompt now asks for an estimate from the axis, flagged `"estimated": true`, and the scorer reports prices within 1% as well as within one tick.
* Muse Spark Open read charts as well as Muse Spark 1.3 on this set, at about a minute per chart.
* The Claude rows ran through Claude Code rather than the API at temperature 0; they are a reference point, not part of the pipeline, which runs on Meta models.
* 20 charts is a small set; the next comparison is on real charts.

Reproduce: `scripts/run_feasibility.py --synthetic 20 --provider meta|baseten|openai|anthropic [--model ID]`.
